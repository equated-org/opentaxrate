Design plan · accuracy pass and benchmark · branch debug-rates (PR #4) · 2026-10-22

Answer what the data supports, refuse the rest, and measure it on addresses nobody tuned on

OpenTaxRate refused about one address in ten that the state's data could settle, mostly rural points the geocoders disagree on and places with no outline of their own. This branch fixes the bugs behind those refusals without guessing, and the plan builds a benchmark of random easy and hard addresses with a sealed evaluation set, so accuracy claims can't come from tuning to the test.

Traced from src/geocoding/locator.ts, src/geocoding/agreement.ts, src/rates/{index,map,ranges}.ts, src/ingest/states/montana.ts, the bench in scripts/bench.ts, stored answers in artifacts/bench/answers/, read-only queries on the live database (2026-10-21/22), a Codex review of the branch, and web research cited in place.

Why

A lookup has three steps: read the street, place the address on a map when the street alone can't settle the rate, and stack the rates of the outlines it falls in. The engine refuses whenever it can't be sure, and that rule stays. What this pass found is that many refusals were not uncertainty in the data but bugs in how the evidence was read: a strict street-name match threw away correct geocodes, a tie rule refused providers that agreed, and a margin rule widened a precise point because a coarse one sat inside its own error.

The goal is the user's: fix legitimate bugs, not benchmark-max. So every change below is a rule that would be right on an address the bench never saw, and the plan's second half builds a sealed evaluation set to keep it that way.

  • Safety first. A wrong rate is worse than a refusal: every change was checked for new wrong answers, and two that produced them were narrowed or reverted.
  • Out of scope. Taxability, sourcing and caps stay the caller's; this is the general rate at an address.

How a lookup is placed now

Figure 1 follows one address the old code refused: 3030 Courtney Gr, Perry FL 32348. Four datasets found it, 34 to 219 m apart (Mapbox, Geocodio, Pelias on OpenAddresses, Census). None agreed within its margin, so the old locator returned nothing. Now it returns the most precise point with a circle reaching every other point plus that point's own margin. Taylor County has one rate everywhere inside that circle, so the engine answers 7%, the state's answer.

Rules

  • No point is better than a wrong point. A circle is never smaller than the evidence: each other dataset's distance plus its own margin, capped at 10 km.
  • Datasets, not providers, vote. Geocodio's TIGER interpolations are Census's data; Nominatim, Photon and Pelias on OSM are one dataset.
  • A point outside the typed city's outline is evidence against it. The ZIP check applies only where the city has no outline of its own.
  • A looked-up ZIP+4 is a lead, a typed one is a fact. A ZIP+4 from standardization answers unless the map contradicts it.
  • An unconfirmed rate is refused inside its outline and near its edge. Outside every such outline the base rate stands.
  • Data is read at sync, not listed in code. Special areas, their names and their outlines come from each state's seed.

Codex review

Codex reviewed the branch diff (run through the Codex CLI; pi has no provider logged in yet). Its five findings were real, and all five were fixed before this page was written:

  1. Refinement could erase a disagreement. The wide placement didn't record its points, so sharpen() could pick one of them as a precise answer. It now records them, and a refinement must agree with all.
  2. The circle omitted the other points' own margins. A point 300 m away with a 250 m margin needs 550 m. Fixed in locator.ts.
  3. "Two datasets" counted providers. Census plus Geocodio-on-TIGER could overrule a state file. Now distinct datasets.
  4. The ZIP check overrode a contradicting city. insideTypedCity now answers inside, outside or no outline.
  5. West Yellowstone's rate. Confirmed against the town's own page; Montana rates are no longer used.

It also suggested carrying dataset identity as structured data rather than parsing display labels, and testing adversarial cases (shared-source providers, competing clusters, refinement after disagreement). Both are in the phases below.

Data gaps and PostGIS

The stored geometry is sound: read-only checks found 0 invalid and 0 empty outlines among about 17,600 across 42 states, all SRID 4326. Storing NAD83 TIGER data as WGS84 shifts it about 1–2 m in the lower 48, far below geocoder error. Simplifying each outline on its own at 1e-5° (about 1 m) leaves slivers between neighbours (83 county pairs in Alabama, 2.6 ha in total), which sit inside the 2 m probe across an edge. What's missing matters more:

  • Alabama police jurisdictions. No published outlines; Act 2021-297 froze each at its 2021 extent, and ALDOR posts reductions as PDFs. Buffering 2021 TIGER places by 1.5 or 3 miles less city limits and posted reductions would settle most (3 held-out refusals).
  • Annexations lag in TIGER. tl_2026 is as of 1 January and voluntary. State layers already used as oracles (SD DOR, NE, TN, SC, Iowa) could be loaded as outlines; in South Dakota, TIGER sided with the DOR in 23 of 26 disagreements with the SST file.
  • Old SST files. Iowa, Nevada, West Virginia, North Carolina and South Dakota files are the newest posted but months old; a staleness check against each state's own rate-change list would catch missed changes.
  • Overlapping city outlines in state layers. Texas (Weslaco/Donna 15.9 ha) and Colorado (Arvada/Westminster 4.6 ha): confirm stacking refuses or follows the state's precedence.
  • Hawaii and tribal areas. Hawaii's 4.712% is the maximum pass-on, not the tax; the Navajo Nation's 6% isn't in Arizona's tables. Both should be documented.

No rules in code: seeds per state

A state's special rules belong in its seed, the adapter under src/ingest/states/ that runs at refresh, and should be read from the state's own sources rather than listed in code. Montana now works this way: montana.ts reads the Department of Revenue's resort table (name, rate, year enacted), matches each area to its outline (a town's own outline, else the resort districts named for it), and stores both in PostGIS: the outline as a district shape and the rate as a resort-tax row. Inside an area the engine answers the table's rate, near its edge it refuses, and outside every area it is 0%. Craig and Wolf Creek publish no outline and are refused by name. The router learns which cities are resort areas from those rows, so statewide.ts no longer lists any. On Montana's 46 bench cases this gives 43 right, 0 wrong, 3 refused. Two lists remain in code and should move the same way:

  • Statewide rates. statewide.ts holds DE, NH, OR, CT, ME, MD, MA and DC; each could be read from the state's published rate at sync, with an end date where one is announced (DC's rate is set through 2027-09-30).
  • Missouri's ZIP+4 rule. UNCONFIRMED_ZIP4_STATES in ranges.ts should be a flag row Missouri's seed writes, with the evidence in its comment.

Benchmark dataset

The current bench grew from cases each session chose, and the rules above were tuned on its first five addresses per state. To measure honestly, the plan builds a new pool of random addresses, labels them once, and seals part of it.

"Random" here has a precise meaning: for each state, a uniform draw by row id, with a fixed seed, from the USDOT National Address Database (about 98 million official address points), and from OpenStreetMap where NAD has no data (HI, MI, NH, NV) or covers only a few counties (FL, GA, LA, SC, SD). The draw is reproducible and documented in pool/README.md. Random addresses are not weighted by where sales happen, and a third-party "random address" generator would add an unverifiable method and licence without fixing either.

  1. Sample. 40 addresses per state (2,040), excluding the existing cases, written to artifacts/bench/pool/candidates.csv: 1,380 easy and 660 hard.
  2. Tag. Hard means within 150 m of a county, city or district line, in a split ZIP, unincorporated next to a special district, or a lettered or fractional house number.
  3. Split. Dev batches 1–5 at 14% each, eval-A and eval-B at 15% each, by sha256("split1|" + id) within each state and difficulty.
  4. Label. The state's own rate where it can be fetched automatically, then vendor answers, one batch at a time within budget. Everything lands in artifacts/bench/answers/ in the format scripts/bench.ts reads.
  5. Score. Right, wrong and refused per batch; the eval sets only when a change is ready.

Official-rate labels

The truth label should be the state's own published answer. For each state the pool records its oracle: an official address lookup API (Washington DOR, the Texas Comptroller's lat/long rate service, California's CDTFA), a GIS rate layer (South Dakota, Nebraska, Utah's sales tax areas), or a published rate table matched to an official boundary. States with no automatable oracle are labelled only by agreement between vendors and marked as weaker evidence. artifacts/bench/pool/OFFICIAL-ORACLES.md lists which states are automatable.

Vendor answers and their shapes

Batch 1 (286 addresses) is labelled by Avalara, Lovat and TaxJar's sandbox; TaxCloud v1 answers only in the 8 states its account collects in (AZ, IL, MO, MS, NY, PA, TX, VA). Budgets stay under 80% of each account's credits. Zip-Tax has used 73 of 100 lifetime requests, so it sits this round out. TaxJar's sandbox token returns the same rates and doesn't bill. Avalara is a free trial with no reported limit, so it gets a fixed number of calls per batch. TaxCloud v1 works but returns $0 outside the states the account collects in.

Price per 1,000 lookups

About 45% of lookups need a map point. The locator asks a free pair first and paid providers only when the free ones can't settle it; scaling the bench's call counts (Census 329, OSM 300 to 330, Geocodio about 300 including standardization, HERE 129, Mapbox 75, Google 27 per 1,000 lookups) to list prices gives:

Open questions

  • Montana's table rates. They are now used, with the table named in each answer's source. The Department says the table may be out of date, and West Yellowstone's own page says 4% since a 2019 vote where the table says 3%. Recommended: a seed check that reads each area's own page where one exists and fails the refresh on a mismatch, rather than a correction in code.
  • Keep Missouri's ZIP+4 exception? Recommended: yes, as a seed flag with its evidence, until Missouri's own address-level data replaces it.
  • Is the 10 km circle cap right? Recommended: keep it, since the engine tests the whole circle, but report how many answers came from circles over 2 km on eval-A.
  • Refuse a street-file answer the map can't confirm in a split ZIP? With open-source geocoders only, two answers are wrong because the state's hundred-block range disagrees with its own lookup (3622 Bellamys Mill Rd, Whitakers NC: 6.75% vs 7%; 6201 Magic Canyon Rd, Rapid City SD: 4.2% vs 6.2%), and Census's point 5 km off makes the circle too wide to confirm. Recommended: measure on eval-A how many right street answers such a rule would refuse before deciding; the precise providers already settle both.
  • TaxCloud as a label? Recommended: only in the states the account collects in, since elsewhere it returns $0.

Appendix: words and concepts this page assumes

Margin

How far from a point the address could really be. A line closer than the margin could put the address on either side.

rooftop 15 m · parcel 25 m · point 40 m · interpolated 75 m
wide: max over others(distance + their margin), e.g. 300 + 250 = 550 m

Dataset vs provider

Providers are services; datasets are where their points come from. Agreement only counts between datasets.

census (tiger) + geocodio (tiger)      → 1 dataset
mapbox (mapbox) + google (google)      → 2 datasets

Hundred-block range

A state file's row for a run of house numbers on one street, filed by the hundred. A block can cross a city or county line.

BELLAMY MILL RD 3600–3699 both sides → one area (Nash County)
3622 is in Edgecombe County: 7.0%, not 6.75%

ZCTA

The Census Bureau's approximation of a ZIP code's area, used here only as a plausibility check for a lone point.

GET geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder/geographies/coordinates
  ?x=-158.2&y=21.4&layers=all → "2020 Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas": 96792

Held-out and sealed sets

Addresses never used to choose a rule. Scoring on them shows whether a change generalizes or just fits the cases it was built on.

split = sha256("split1|" + id) mod 100 within (state, difficulty)
0–69 dev batches 1–5 · 70–84 eval-A · 85–99 eval-B

"no" rows

A rate row that says a jurisdiction exists but its rate can't be given. Inside its outline, or within the margin of it, the address is refused.

MT | no | WEST_YELLOWSTONE | 0.03 (state table, unconfirmed)
→ "This address is in West Yellowstone, which levies its own tax ..."
